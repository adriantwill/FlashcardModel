Clinical Application:
Valvular Heart Disease
• Stenosis – narrowing and failure of a valve 
to open fully, slowing blood flow from a 
chamber
• Insufficiency (aka regurgitation or 
incompetence) - failure of the valve to 
close, allowing blood to flow back into the 
chamber it was ejected from
• Murmurs – audible vibrations caused by the 
turbulent blood flow of  valvular stenosis or 
insufficiency
• Damaged or defective heart valves can be 
replaced surgically with prosthetic valves

California Northstate University College of Dental Medicine
Clinical Application: 
Infective Endocarditis
• Infection caused by bacteria that enter the 
bloodstream and settle in the endocardium 
(inner lining of the heart), heart valves or blood 
vessels
• Tissue that experiences turbulent blood flow 
most at risk (valves), mitral and aortic valve 
most commonly affected
• Can be caused by poor oral hygiene, 
toothbrushing causing minor injury in the 
mouth, dental procedures
• Symptoms: fever, heart murmur, 
tachycardia, cough, aching joints/muscles

Cardiac Referred Pain

5
29
•
Ischemia (lack of oxygen) as in angina 
pectoris or a myocardial infarction can 
stimulate pain fibers in the heart
•
Pain fibers run centrally in middle and 
inferior cervical branches and especially 
in the thoracic cardiac branches of the 
sympathetic trunk
•
Axons enter spinal cord segments T1 
through T4/5 especially on the left side
•
Presents as referred pain to the 
superficial somatic area supplied by the 
spinal nerves involved
•
Most commonly the left shoulder, arm 
and chest

Clinical Application: Pneumothorax
• Air introduced into pleural cavity
• Lung collapses due to loss of negative 
pressure in the pleural cavity
• Injury: gunshot or knife wound, fractured 
rib, air blisters of the lung break open as in 
scuba diving or travel to high altitude
• Leads to pneumothorax
• Symptoms: shortness of breath, painful 
respiration
• Hemothorax – blood in pleural cavity
• Hydrothorax – excess fluid in pleural cavity
